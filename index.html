The AppliedAI Lab

A project by Daniel Blaseg (more collaborators to be announced soon).

A place for Esade students and professors who already build with AI to meet peers and push their AI skills further.

After signup, choose your interests, preferred formats and ways to take part.

Inside the Lab

You might teach a session on an AI tool you know well and join the next to learn from someone in a different field.

We’re planning up to two free Lab events a month.

Build together

  • Pitch and build

    Hear a few short workflow pitches, then take one apart with its creator and adapt it to a task of your own.

    Example Automate your job search

    Set your target roles and locations, then try the workflow on a real vacancy. Use examples from your own work to tailor your CV and cover letter.

  • Hands-on build sessions

    Work on a topic members choose by vote, or bring your own project. A host and other members can help when you get stuck.

    Example Fine-tune a model to classify expenses

    Use LoRA to train a small model on sample expense claims. Give it a hotel bill that includes a room, dinner and parking, then check how it classifies each charge.

    Example Create a professional headshot

    Bring a few photos of yourself. Try different lighting and framing, then choose a portrait that still looks like you.

  • Hackathons and competitions

    Build in teams and pitch the result to the people who would use it.

    Example Venture capital hackathon

    Your fund can meet one of three fictional startups. Build an AI tool that checks claims in their pitch decks against customer interviews, then defend your choice. Judges test each team’s tool on a fresh case.

Talks and visits

  • Talks by researchers

    Hear directly from researchers about their latest findings on AI and the questions they’re exploring next.

    Example What can we do with a model’s internal representations?

    Daniel Blaseg shares his research on the patterns of activity inside language models. How can we use them to compare ideas across texts or study bias? What happens to an answer when we change one of those patterns?

  • Talks by practitioners

    Founders, alumni and other practitioners share how organisations adopt AI, what changes in day-to-day work and how they handle governance.

    Example Who decides when an AI pilot can go live?

    Follow a tool from a small team trial to wider use. The person behind the rollout explains who approved it, how staff could challenge a decision and who could pause it.

  • Lectures and best-practice sessions

    Guest experts share insights from their field, drawing on research and examples from practice.

    Example The science of slide design in the age of AI

    Half the room sees an AI-generated slide. The other half sees the same data with a different headline and chart. Compare what each group remembers, then hear what research on attention and comprehension can teach us about slide design.

  • Field visits

    Meet Barcelona teams where they work, when a company or lab can host us.

    Example Visit the Barcelona Supercomputing Center

    We’d request a group visit to see MareNostrum 5 and learn what it takes to run AI models on a supercomputer.

Between sessions

  • Newsletter

    A short email with a workflow to try, what members learned testing it, and the next Lab dates.

  • Curated database

    A searchable collection of AI tools, skills and workflows, from slide design to Moodle quizzes. Each entry includes setup notes and the date a Lab member last tested it.

About

Daniel Blaseg

I’m an associate professor of entrepreneurship at Esade. In our AI bootcamps, I saw how much experienced users learned when they helped someone else try a workflow for the first time.

That’s why I’m proposing the Lab. I’d like students and professors across programmes to have a place to bring unfinished work and teach each other what they’ve figured out.
